Teaching and mentoring

Build physical intuition, then test it

I teach quantitative subjects by connecting equations to observable behavior. Students first make a physical prediction, then test it with a calculation, model, experiment, or dataset. The goal is not to hide complexity. It is to make each assumption visible enough that students can question it and eventually work independently.

Start with the process

Before introducing a numerical method, I ask students to describe what water, force, or mass should do. Diagrams, limiting cases, and conservation laws give them a way to check the result.

Make models understandable

I use open code, spreadsheets, and worked examples so students can change assumptions and see the consequences. A model becomes a learning tool when students can examine how it works.

Move toward ownership

Mentoring begins with clear milestones and frequent feedback. As students gain confidence, I shift from answers to questions, code review, and decisions they must defend.
