This paper examines how nonstationary rainfall can shift urban floods beyond the design conditions assumed for drainage infrastructure in Sao Paulo State. It propagates changes in rainfall inputs through hydrologic calculations to evaluate flood escalation and design consequences. The study shows why updating rainfall statistics is necessary when the intended performance of infrastructure extends into a changing climate.
